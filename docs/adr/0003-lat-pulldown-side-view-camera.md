# Camera placement is chosen per Exercise; the lat pulldown is filmed from the side

The thesis picks a 30° diagonal view as a compromise between the side view (knee lockout) and the front view (knee valgus). That argument is about the leg exercises only, so camera placement is decided **per Exercise** instead. The lat pulldown is filmed from the user's **side** (90° from the front, accepted within ±30° of side-on). The user can turn either side to the camera and places their own phone in portrait: lens at about seated shoulder height (1.0–1.2 m), 2–3 m away, everything from the knees to the hands at full overhead extension in frame. The app gives these instructions but does not check framing. The side view is the only one where torso leaning, the walking skeleton's single Postural error, stands out clearly. Bar behind the neck, the next candidate, also needs a side or oblique view.

## Considered Options

- **Front view**: rejected. Torso leaning is invisible from the front: on the reference recordings, leaning Reps measured within 1–3° of correct ones.
- **Oblique, 30–45° from the front**: rejected. Foreshortening pushes leaning Reps down to about 15°, right at the threshold (correct Reps measured 3–5°).
- **Camera mounted by the gym or instructor** (the thesis's wording): deferred to deployment. For now the user films with their own phone.

## Consequences

- Torso leaning is judged against the starting posture of the Set's **first Rep**. A Rep is incorrect when the torso leans more than **15°** beyond that posture during the Rep. The 15° is a starting value, calibrated only for the side view, and gets tuned later on recordings. Measuring from each Rep's own start was rejected: it misses a user who gets tired and stays leaned back between Reps.
- Thresholds are only valid for the view they were calibrated on. Changing the placement means re-calibrating them.
- Evidence (side view): correct Reps measured 0–1° of extra lean, leaning Reps 21–30°.
